Questions, answered

Everything an operator asks before plugging us in.

Setup & integration
Does this change how my players deposit?

No. Players deposit exactly as they do today. You send us the deposit event over a webhook or API call, and we hand back the verdict. Nothing about your cashier, your wallets, or the player experience changes.

What do I actually have to send you?

The deposit event: the wallet address, the chain, the amount, a timestamp, and your own player reference. That is enough for us to read the wallet and return value and risk. Your player reference can stay pseudonymous if you prefer.

Do I have to replace my platform, CRM, or AML stack?

No. Whal3Core sits alongside whatever you already run. It does not migrate data or take over any function. Run it standalone next to your current platform, or as the wallet-intelligence layer inside WAG3R.

How long does it take to go live?

You can be live in weeks, not quarters. Once your deposit events are connected, we spend roughly one to two weeks calibrating the engine to your geos and seeding the cross-casino registry, then you go live on every deposit.

How do we act on a verdict once it is live?

Three ways, usually together: the full dossier for your ops team, scores piped into your CRM for segmentation and automation, and alerts into Telegram so the right person sees a whale or a risk the moment funds land.

Data, privacy & security
Do you ever touch or custody our funds?

Never. Whal3Core is read-only intelligence. We never hold, move, or have access to funds, keys, or wallets. We read public on-chain data and return a verdict, nothing more.

What player data do you store?

We work from the wallet, not the person. We read public blockchain data plus the deposit event you send. We do not need player names, emails, or documents, and your player reference can be pseudonymous. Your data stays yours, and we never sell or share it.

Is this GDPR friendly?

Designed with it in mind. You remain the controller of any player data you send, we act as your processor, and we can sign a data processing agreement. Because we lean on public on-chain data and a reference you control, the personal-data footprint is deliberately small.

Where does the intelligence come from, and how current is it?

From our own blockchain nodes and an attribution layer we maintain ourselves, with no third-party data dependency. We map roughly 90% of active on-chain addresses across the supported chains, current to the chain tip, so a verdict reflects the wallet as it stands at the moment of deposit.

How the intelligence works
How accurate is it?

The verdict is a probability and a risk read, not a guarantee, and every dossier carries a confidence level so your team knows how much weight to give it. Accuracy sharpens once we calibrate to your geos and player base. We would rather tell you confidence is low than hand you a number we cannot stand behind.

What happens with a brand-new or thin wallet?

A wallet with little history gives us less to read, so we return a lower confidence instead of inventing a verdict. As that wallet builds a footprint, later reads get sharper. We are explicit about this rather than pretending every wallet is fully knowable.

How do you know which other casinos a player uses?

From the on-chain footprint. We see funds moving to and from wallets we have attributed to other crypto casinos, and cross-reference them against our registry. That is what powers the cross-casino view and your share of a player's wallet.

Can a player beat it by using fresh wallets?

A determined player can split funds across wallets, and we are honest that a clean, freshly funded wallet is the hardest case. We counter it by tracing funding hops and surfacing linked accounts that share on-chain infrastructure, which is how multi-accounting and wallet farms tend to give themselves away.

Is the VIP score the same as the risk score?

No, and we never blend them. Value and risk are scored separately and shown side by side, because a player can be both highly valuable and high risk at once. Collapsing them into one number hides the exact cases you most need to see.

Scope: what it does and does not do
Is this just another AML tool?

No. It does the AML work in full: source of funds, sanctions and mixer exposure, with risk scored on every deposit. The difference is it does not stop there. The same read also tells you who the player is worth, across every casino, from the wallet, so one verdict drives both compliance and revenue. AML-only tools answer whether funds are dirty; we answer that and who the player is.

Does it replace Chainalysis, Elliptic, or TRM?

Not really, because they do a different primary job: compliance and forensics. We overlap on the source-of-funds read, but our reason to exist is player value and cross-casino intelligence, which they do not provide. You can run a compliance suite and Whal3Core side by side; they solve different problems.
